This glossary gives the technical vocabulary used throughout the site in English, with the Italian form that appears in the primary sources and in most of the specialist literature. Where a term is best understood from a specific work, an example points to the relevant entry. For the dating conventions that govern the reading of medieval Italian documents and inscriptions, see [Dating Systems](/dating-systems/).

Claims are marked according to their evidential status:

- **[D]** documented — attested in a primary source
- **[C]** scholarly consensus
- **[?]** contested or variable — practice differs by period, chancery or scribe
- **[T]** tradition without documentary support

## Architecture

**Ambulatory** (*deambulatorio*) — The aisle that continues around the apse behind the high altar, permitting circulation and, in pilgrimage churches, access to radiating chapels.

**Apse** (*abside*) — The semicircular or polygonal termination of a church, normally at the east end. Smaller subsidiary apses are *absidiole*.

**Baldachin / ciborium** (*ciborio*) — A canopy on columns over an altar or a tomb. In Italian usage *ciborio* also denotes the vessel for the reserved Host; context decides.

**Bifora, trifora, monofora** — Windows divided by one, two or no colonnettes respectively. The terminology is standard in Italian scholarship and has no exact single-word English equivalent.

**Blind arcading** (*arcate cieche*) — Arches applied to a wall surface without opening through it.

**Clerestory** (*cleristorio*) — The upper storey of the nave wall, pierced by windows, rising above the aisle roofs.

**Corbel** (*peduccio*, *mensola*) — A projecting block supporting a vault rib, cornice or arch. A continuous run of small corbels beneath a cornice forms a corbel table.

**Cosmatesque** (*cosmatesco*) — Geometric inlay of coloured marble and glass tesserae, characteristic of Rome and Lazio from the twelfth century, named after the Cosmati family of marble workers. **[C]** The term is a modern coinage and is applied more broadly than the family's documented output warrants. **[?]**

**Crypt** (*cripta*) — Subterranean or semi-subterranean space beneath the presbytery, normally housing relics.

**Lesene and archetti pensili** (*lesene*, *archetti pensili*) — Flat vertical strips and the small hanging arches that link them, together forming the decorative system usually called **Lombard bands**. Diagnostic of Romanesque building across northern Italy and beyond.

**Matroneum** (*matroneo*) — A gallery above the aisle, opening onto the nave. The traditional identification of this space as reserved for women is a **[T]** claim; the functional evidence is thin and varies by region.

**Narthex** (*nartece*, *endonartece*) — The vestibule preceding the nave. An open colonnaded forecourt is an *atrio*.

**Pendentive and squinch** (*pennacchio*, *tromba*) — The two solutions for setting a circular dome over a square bay: a spherical triangle, or a small arch or niche bridging the corner.

**Protyrum** (*protiro*) — A projecting porch before a portal, often carried on columns resting on lions (*leoni stilofori*, "column-bearing lions"). Characteristic of Lombard and Emilian Romanesque.

**Rood screen** (*tramezzo*, *pontile*, *iconostasi*) — The barrier separating the lay nave from the clergy's choir. In Italy the *tramezzo* was widely dismantled in the sixteenth century; its former presence is often recoverable only from wall scars, floor traces and payment records. **[D]** in individual cases, but reconstruction of its exact form is normally **[?]**.

**Splayed portal** (*portale strombato*) — A doorway whose jambs recede in receding orders, typically filled with colonnettes and figural sculpture; the semicircular field above the lintel is the **tympanum** (*lunetta*), the moulded arches framing it the **archivolts** (*archivolti*).

**Tiburio** — The external casing, usually octagonal, enclosing a dome or crossing vault. A characteristically Lombard feature with no standard English term.

**Transenna** and **pluteus** (*pluteo*) — Perforated screen panel and solid parapet slab respectively; the surviving carved fragments of early medieval liturgical furnishing are very often of these two types.

## Sculpture

**Ambo** and **pulpit** (*ambone*, *pergamo*, *pulpito*) — The raised platform for the reading of scripture. *Ambone* is normally used for the earlier, often paired, fixed structures; *pergamo* for the freestanding polygonal pulpits of the thirteenth and fourteenth centuries. The freestanding polygonal type reaches its fullest development in the pulpits of [Nicola Pisano](/Content/Artists/XIII-c/Nicola-Pisano.html) and [Giovanni Pisano](/Content/Artists/XIII-c/Giovanni-Pisano.html), where narrative reliefs, figured supports and stylophore lions form a single architectural-sculptural whole.

**Arca** — A monumental tomb-chest, especially one raised on columns and containing the relics of a saint. The type is established by the Arca di San Domenico at Bologna, designed in the workshop of [Nicola Pisano](/Content/Artists/XIII-c/Nicola-Pisano.html).

**Atlas / telamon** (*telamone*, *atlante*) — A male figure serving as a support; the female equivalent is a caryatid. A crouching telamon carries one of the columns of the Pistoia pulpit by [Giovanni Pisano](/Content/Artists/XIII-c/Giovanni-Pisano.html).

**Capital types** — *Corinthian* (acanthus, classical derivation); *cubic* or *cushion* (*capitello cubico*, a cube intersected by a hemisphere, typical of eleventh-century Lombard and Ottonian building); *crocket* (*a crochet*, with bud-like volutes, Gothic); *historiated* (*istoriato*, carrying narrative figures).

**Relief** — *altorilievo* (high relief), *bassorilievo* (low relief). The extremely shallow pictorial relief later associated with Donatello (*stiacciato* or *schiacciato*) belongs to the fifteenth century and should not be applied retrospectively. For relief conceived in painterly terms — composition related to an ideal surface rather than to the depth of the block — see the Orsanmichele tabernacle of [Andrea di Cione (Orcagna)](/Content/Artists/XIV-c/Andrea-di-Cione-Orcagna.html).

## Painting and Panel Construction

**Arriccio, intonaco, sinopia, giornata, pontata** — The technical sequence of true fresco: the coarse first plaster layer; the fine plaster receiving the paint; the preparatory drawing in red earth on the *arriccio*; the area of *intonaco* laid for a single day's work; the horizontal band corresponding to a level of scaffolding. Counting *giornate* is one of the few objective means of reconstructing the sequence and division of labour in a fresco cycle. **[C]**

**Bole** (*bolo armeno*) — The red clay ground laid beneath gold leaf for burnishing; its warm tone shows through worn gilding.

**Buon fresco** and ***a secco*** — Pigment bound by carbonation into wet plaster, versus pigment applied with a binder onto dry plaster. Blues (azurite, ultramarine) and greens were commonly *a secco* and have often been lost, which distorts our perception of original colour balance. **[C]**

**Croce dipinta** — The monumental painted cross. Two iconographic types are conventionally distinguished: ***Christus triumphans***, the living Christ with open eyes, dominant to the earlier thirteenth century, and ***Christus patiens***, the dead Christ with closed eyes and curved body, ascendant thereafter. **[C]** The chronological neatness of this succession is a modern schematisation and admits many exceptions. **[?]** Component parts: the lateral panels (*tabelloni*), the terminal at the head (*cimasa*), the plate beneath the feet (*suppedaneo*). For the earlier type see [Alberto Sozio](/Content/Artists/XII-c/Alberto-Sozio.html); for the later, [Giunta Pisano](/Content/Artists/XIII-c/Giunta-Pisano.html) and [Cimabue](/Content/Artists/XIII-c/Cimabue.html).

**Gold ground** (*fondo oro*) — Gold leaf laid over the whole background field. Surface decoration is produced by **punching** (*punzonatura*) with iron tools; punch-mark inventories are a recognised, if contested, tool of workshop attribution. **[?]**

**Maestà** — The Virgin and Child enthroned in majesty amid saints and angels, especially in the monumental panel and fresco tradition of Tuscany. See [Duccio di Buoninsegna](/Content/Artists/XIII-c/Duccio-di-Buoninsegna.html) and [Cimabue](/Content/Artists/XIII-c/Cimabue.html).

**Polyptych, dossal, predella** (*polittico*, *dossale*, *predella*) — A multi-panel altarpiece; a horizontal altarpiece behind the altar; the low base register beneath the main panels, usually carrying narrative scenes. The wooden framing structure is the *carpenteria*, and its dismemberment is the single commonest reason why panels of one complex are now dispersed across several collections. **[D]** The Strozzi altarpiece of [Andrea di Cione (Orcagna)](/Content/Artists/XIV-c/Andrea-di-Cione-Orcagna.html) is unusual in suppressing the dividing colonnettes, unifying the compartments into a single field.

**Tempera** — Pigment bound in egg yolk, the standard medium for Italian panel painting in this period.

## Manuscripts and Codicology

**Bifolium, quire, recto/verso** (*bifolio*, *fascicolo*, *recto*/*verso*) — A sheet folded once to give two leaves; a gathering of such sheets sewn together (a *quaternion* is four bifolia, eight leaves); the front and back of a leaf. Foliation cites leaf and side: f. 76v.

**Canon tables** (*tavole dei canoni*) — Eusebius's concordance of the Gospels, set out under arcades in the opening quires of Gospel books. See the [Codex Aureus of Echternach](/Content/Codex/Codex-Aureus-of-Echternach.html).

**Incipit, explicit, colophon** — The opening words; the closing formula; the scribe's terminal note, which may record name, place and date. Colophons are the closest thing to direct documentation a manuscript offers — and are also the element most often copied verbatim from an exemplar, so a colophon dates the exemplar unless independently corroborated. **[?]** For a monogram incipit page carried to monumental scale, see the MARCUS opening of the [Codex Aureus of Echternach](/Content/Codex/Codex-Aureus-of-Echternach.html).

**Initials** — *decorated* (ornamental only), *inhabited* (*abitata*, containing figures or animals without narrative content), *historiated* (*istoriata*, containing an identifiable scene). The distinction is analytically important and is frequently blurred in older catalogues.

**Palimpsest** (*palinsesto*) — Parchment scraped and reused; the erased lower text is recoverable by multispectral imaging.

**Pricking and ruling** (*foratura*, *rigatura*) — The marginal holes and the ruled lines guiding the script. Ruling patterns are a primary criterion for localising and dating undated codices. **[C]**

**Purple parchment and chrysography** — Parchment dyed purple and script written in gold or silver, reserved for the most prestigious commissions. The [Codex Aureus of Echternach](/Content/Codex/Codex-Aureus-of-Echternach.html) takes its name from the practice.

**Scripts** — *uncial*, *half-uncial*, *insular*, *Merovingian*, *Beneventan* (southern Italy, Montecassino sphere), *Caroline minuscule*, *Gothic textualis*, *rotunda* (the rounded Italian Gothic bookhand). For insular script and ornament see the [Lindisfarne Gospels](/Content/Codex/Lindisfarne-Gospels.html) and the [Lichfield Gospels](/Content/Codex/Lichfield-Gospels.html).

## Iconography

**Anastasis** — The Byzantine Resurrection image, Christ raising Adam and Eve from Hades; the Western "Harrowing of Hell" is its counterpart.

**Deesis** — Christ flanked by the Virgin and the Baptist in intercession. The formula appears at the centre of the Strozzi altarpiece by [Andrea di Cione (Orcagna)](/Content/Artists/XIV-c/Andrea-di-Cione-Orcagna.html).

**Maiestas Domini** — Christ enthroned in a mandorla, normally with the **Tetramorph**, the four evangelist symbols (man, lion, ox, eagle). See the [Codex Aureus of Echternach](/Content/Codex/Codex-Aureus-of-Echternach.html).

**Mandorla** — The almond-shaped glory enclosing a figure in majesty or in transit between earthly and divine spheres.

**Nimbus** (*nimbo*) — The halo; the *cruciform nimbus* (*nimbo crucigero*), bearing a cross, is reserved to the persons of the Trinity.

**Pantocrator, Theotokos, Hodegetria, Eleousa** — Byzantine formulations widely diffused in Italy: Christ the all-ruler; the Mother of God; the Virgin indicating the Child with her right hand; the Virgin of tenderness, cheek against the Child's.

**Traditio legis** — Christ delivering the Law to Peter in the presence of Paul, a formula of Late Antique and early medieval Roman origin. Its afterlife in the Trecento is visible in the Strozzi altarpiece of [Andrea di Cione (Orcagna)](/Content/Artists/XIV-c/Andrea-di-Cione-Orcagna.html), where Christ hands the book to Thomas Aquinas and the keys to Peter.
